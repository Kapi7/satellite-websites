The lowest price per screw is not always the cheapest way to finish a project. Compare the specification first, then calculate both unit price and the amount you must spend to obtain enough suitable pieces.

## Compare the same specification

Record the required fastener type, dimensions, thread, material and finish. Check compatibility with the material or assembly in your project. A cheaper pack with the wrong specification is not a substitute. For anchors or other load-bearing fixings, follow the manufacturer’s requirements for the substrate and installation; a headline load rating alone does not establish suitability.

## Calculate two prices

**Unit price = pack price ÷ number of suitable pieces in the pack.**

**Purchase cost = number of packs required × pack price**, plus applicable delivery and other checkout charges. Round the pack count up to a whole pack.

For mixed assortments, count the pieces that match your requirement. Do not divide by the headline total if most pieces are unsuitable for the job.

| Hypothetical option | Suitable pieces per pack | Pack price | Unit price | Cost to obtain at least 30 pieces |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Small pack | 20 | $4 | $0.20 | 2 packs: $8 |
| Large pack | 100 | $12 | $0.12 | 1 pack: $12 |

These are invented numbers to demonstrate the calculation, not retail quotations. With identical suitable pieces and no delivery difference, the small packs cost $4 less for this job. The large pack has a lower unit price but leaves 70 pieces after using 30, versus 10 left from the small packs.

## Use this shopping checklist

- [ ] Confirm the exact specification and quantity needed.
- [ ] Check whether washers, nuts or other components are included or separate.
- [ ] Count suitable pieces rather than all pieces in an assortment.
- [ ] Round up the number of packs required.
- [ ] Compare the complete checkout cost.
- [ ] Decide whether you have a real use and storage space for the surplus.

If a kit lacks a required component, add the cost of obtaining it before comparing totals. If you already own suitable supplies, subtract those from the quantity you need to buy.

## Make the decision for this project

Keep a larger pack on the shortlist when you have a credible need for its contents. Otherwise, paying more per piece can still mean spending less overall. Save the specification and receipt with your project notes so you can identify the same item later.
