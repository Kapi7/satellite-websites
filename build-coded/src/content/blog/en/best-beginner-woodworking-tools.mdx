## Plan the work before choosing a brand

| Task | Start by checking | What can wait |
|---|---|---|
| Measure and mark | A readable tape or rule, pencil and appropriate square | Specialty layout tools without a planned use |
| Hold the work | A stable work surface and clamps sized for the workpiece | A large clamp collection bought without dimensions |
| Cut material | Whether the supplier can provide the specified cuts | Your own power saw until you can use and support it safely |
| Drill and fasten | Material, hole sizes, fasteners and matching bits | An impact driver if occasional screws are the only need |
| Prepare the finish | Finish instructions, suitable abrasives and dust control | A powered sander for a tiny one-off job |

For a first small assembly using pre-cut boards, paying for suitable cuts can avoid an immediate saw purchase. Check the supplier's accuracy and available dimensions before designing around that service. Tools, fixings and workholding still need to fit the particular plan.

## Decide whether you need a drill, a battery or a whole kit

A bare cordless tool is useful only if you already have compatible power equipment or have budgeted for it. The [manufacturer's DCD800D2 listing](https://www.dewalt.com/en-us/product/dcd800d2/20v-max-xr-brushless-cordless-12-drilldriver-kit) describes a drill/driver package; it is not an impact driver or hammer drill.

If you have not decided on the tool type, start with [impact driver versus drill](/impact-driver-vs-drill/). Do not buy both merely because they come in a larger bundle.

## Make a complete cost list

Record materials, fixings, bits, workholding, the tool's power requirements and task-appropriate protective equipment. Add delivery and any cutting service. Compare the complete totals rather than a bare-tool headline price. We do not publish a fixed starter-workshop price because the project and regional prices change it substantially.

## Buy only after the setup makes sense

Read the manuals before choosing tools for unfamiliar work. Arrange stable support and appropriate dust management, and obtain instruction for tools you have not used. A shopping list is not training. Postpone a purchase if you cannot identify where and how the work can be done safely.
