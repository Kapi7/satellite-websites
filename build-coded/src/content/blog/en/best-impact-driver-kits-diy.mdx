An impact-driver kit is worth comparing when repeated fastening is the job you need to do. Start with the task and the batteries you already own. A kit discount is not useful if it buys a second charger you do not need or a tool unsuited to the job.

**How this guide was prepared:** This is a manufacturer-source buying checklist. We have not carried out torque, endurance or three-month jobsite tests. The earlier testing and ranking claims were unsupported and have been removed. The photograph shows a real DCF850B bare tool, not the contents of a kit.

## Confirm you want an impact driver

A drill/driver is generally the more flexible first purchase for a mix of holes and occasional screws. An impact driver is a separate fastening tool. Read our [drill-versus-impact comparison](/impact-driver-vs-drill/) before choosing a package. Follow fastener instructions for pilot holes; owning an impact driver does not make those requirements disappear.

Do not use this guide as an automotive wheel-fastening procedure or a reason to substitute an impact driver for an impact wrench or masonry tool.

## An exact example: DCF850B is tool-only

DeWalt's [DCF850B product page](https://www.dewalt.com/en-us/product/dcf850b/atomic-20v-max-14-3-speed-brushless-impact-driver-tool-only) identifies a three-speed, quarter-inch impact driver and lists the tool and belt hook. Battery and charger are sold separately. The manufacturer places it in the ATOMIC range; it is not the XR kit described by the old version of this article.

The [DCF850 manual](https://www.dewalt.com/GLOBALBOM/QU/DCF850B/1/Instruction_Manual/EN/NA473192_DCF850_T1_NA.pdf) specifies the accessory interface and operating instructions. Check impact suitability of accessories, not just whether a bit physically fits.

A retailer photograph showing a battery does not establish that a particular offer includes it. Match the complete model suffix and written contents list.

## Compare packages line by line

| Package detail | What to write down before buying |
|---|---|
| Tool | Exact model and suffix, not just the brand or family |
| Batteries | Included model numbers, quantity and stated capacity |
| Charger | Exact model, required input supply and compatibility |
| Accessories | Bits, holders, belt hook and case only if explicitly included |
| Existing equipment | Which manufacturer-approved batteries and charger you already own |
| Delivered setup | Listing total, delivery and any missing equipment |

For a hypothetical buyer who already owns compatible equipment, compare a bare tool with a kit after accounting for duplicate items. For a first-time buyer, add every item needed for a usable setup. These are decision examples, not current price quotations or a claim that one package always wins.

## Check regional and seller details

A plug adapter does not prove a charger supports your electrical supply. Check the charger label and manufacturer's instructions for the exact package offered. Also compare delivery destination, seller identity, return terms and applicable warranty information. A familiar tool photograph is not enough to settle any of these questions.
