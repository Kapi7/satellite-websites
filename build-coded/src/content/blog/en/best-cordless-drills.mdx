The best cordless drill for your home is one that handles your next jobs without making you buy an unnecessary second battery system. Start with the work, then decide between a bare tool and a kit. A low headline price is not the cost of a usable setup.

**Quick decision:** For mixed drilling and occasional screwdriving, consider a drill/driver. Already have compatible DeWalt batteries and a charger? Compare the DCD800B bare tool. Starting from nothing? Compare the DCD800D2 kit's delivered total. Neither is a hammer drill. [See the exact packages and Amazon links](#compare-drill-packages).

**Our method:** This is a buying framework using manufacturer documentation and checked product listings, not a seven-tool hands-on shootout. We have not measured runtime, drilling speed or long-term durability. Product photos are genuine manufacturer images. This shortlist is narrow; it does not establish that DeWalt beats every competing brand.

## Start with a three-job list

Write down three tasks you actually expect to do. This is more useful than buying for an imagined future workshop.

| Planned work | What to shortlist | What to avoid assuming |
|---|---|---|
| Furniture assembly and occasional pilot holes | Drill/driver with appropriate bits; a hand screwdriver may suffice for small fittings | Every screw needs a powerful cordless tool |
| Repeated long-screw fastening | Consider a dedicated impact driver | A drill and impact driver are the same tool |
| Drilling masonry | Check a suitable hammer-drill or rotary-hammer model against the material and task | The DCD800 includes a hammer function |
| Adding a tool to an existing collection | First check options compatible with your approved battery system | Changing brands is automatically worth the extra battery cost |

For the first two rows, our [drill versus impact driver guide](/impact-driver-vs-drill/) explains the difference. For a hammer-drill comparison, read [DCD800 versus Milwaukee 2904](/dewalt-vs-milwaukee-cordless-drill/). Those tools have different functions, so the brand name alone does not decide the purchase.

## A focused shortlist for drilling and screwdriving

The [DCD800B manufacturer listing](https://www.dewalt.com/product/dcd800b/20v-max-xr-brushless-cordless-12-drilldriver-tool-only) describes a half-inch drill/driver sold without the battery and charger. It belongs on a shortlist when you have decided this tool type fits your work and already own a compatible power setup.

The [DCD800D2 manufacturer listing](https://www.dewalt.com/en-us/product/dcd800d2/20v-max-xr-brushless-cordless-12-drilldriver-kit) packages the DCD800 with batteries, a charger and storage. Compare it when you need those components. The different suffix describes the package; buying the kit does not turn the drill into a hammer drill.

If you already use Milwaukee, Makita, Ryobi or another system, compare a suitable tool on that platform before switching. We are not recommending a brand change without checking the whole cost. If neither DeWalt package fits your jobs or budget, do not buy either simply because it appears here.

Before using “I already own DeWalt batteries” to choose a bare tool, use the [exact battery and charger checklist](/dewalt-dcd800b-vs-dcd800d2/#check-your-exact-battery-and-charger). It distinguishes a documented DCB203/DCB112 setup from equipment you still need to verify, including a charger's input voltage when importing a US kit.

## Compare the amount you will actually pay

Create two baskets on paper: tool plus everything missing, and kit plus anything still missing. Include delivery and tax in each, in the same currency. Count batteries and storage only if you need them. A second charger you will never use is not a saving.

Use the [DCD800B versus DCD800D2 package comparison](/dewalt-dcd800b-vs-dcd800d2/) for exact contents and a calculator that uses your own prices. It does not fetch prices or predict deals.

Also check seller identity, new versus used condition, delivery to your location, return costs and warranty eligibility. The Amazon US listing and manufacturer page can present warranty information differently; confirm the terms of your actual seller's offer. We do not promise stock, a price, or a warranty duration.

## What else needs to be in your first setup?

A tool kit is not necessarily a project kit. Check for the specific drill and driver bits, work-holding equipment and protective equipment your task requires. Do not infer that accessories pictured in a demonstration are included in the box.

Read the [DCD800/DCD805 manual](https://www.dewalt.com/GLOBALBOM/QU/DCD800B/1/Instruction_Manual/EN/NA229093_DCD800_DCD805_T1_NA.pdf) for your exact model. Shared manuals can describe functions on another model; they do not add those functions to yours. Follow the tool, battery, charger and accessory instructions before use.
