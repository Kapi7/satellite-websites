A bare tool is only the cheaper choice if the equipment you already own makes it usable. A kit is only better value if you need its contents. Compare the cost of finishing your actual setup, not the largest discount badge.

**Quick decision:** Have a documented compatible battery and charger? Price the bare tool plus missing accessories. Starting without them? Compare a kit with the same tool. Need a different function, such as hammer drilling? Choose the right tool before comparing packages.

## Make a short inventory first

Write down your next job, the tool function it needs, and the exact model numbers of your batteries and charger. “Same brand” or “looks like it fits” is not a compatibility check. Use the manufacturer's compatibility information for the actual models; do not rely on an adapter or a marketplace title to establish approval.

Also list the accessories you need: for example, drill bits for the material and driver bits for the fasteners. A kit containing a drill, batteries and charger is not necessarily a kit containing everything for your project.

## Compare complete baskets in one currency

Use this calculation for each offer:

**Delivered setup cost = tool or kit + missing compatible power equipment + required accessories + delivery and tax.**

Do not count a spare bag or charger as money saved if you would never have bought it. Conversely, a second battery may matter to your work even if one is enough to switch the tool on. Decide that from your expected use; we have not measured the runtime of these packages.

A hypothetical calculation illustrates the method: a bare-tool basket costing 120 units plus 90 for missing power equipment totals 210 before shared costs. A kit costing 180 units would cost 30 less in that example. These are invented arithmetic inputs, not current retailer prices or a deal claim. ## Check the seller and destination before paying

The links below are Amazon US listings. Confirm shipping to your address, seller identity, item condition, final tax and delivery charges, return terms and the exact package in your basket. If importing a kit, check the charger's actual input-voltage label and manufacturer guidance for your supply; a plug adapter does not establish electrical compatibility.

If you already use another battery platform, compare a suitable tool there before switching brands. If you need masonry drilling, a different tool may be appropriate; buying more batteries does not add a hammer function to the DCD800.
