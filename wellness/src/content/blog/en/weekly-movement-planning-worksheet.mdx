Put movement in the gaps you actually have, not in an imaginary week with no work, travel or family commitments. This worksheet helps you choose an activity, a realistic time slot and a fallback. It is a planning tool, not a personalised exercise prescription or a promise of weight loss.

**Start here:** Pick one activity you already know how to do comfortably. Put it in two or three specific calendar slots, add a shorter option for a busy day, and review the week without treating a missed session as a debt.

## Separate your starting plan from general guidance

The [CDC's adult activity guidance](https://www.cdc.gov/physical-activity-basics/adding-adults/index.html) describes at least 150 minutes of moderate-intensity activity and two days of muscle-strengthening activity each week. Activity can be divided into smaller periods, and some activity is better than none. A short starting schedule is a way to organise your time; it is not automatically the full recommendation.

Choose activities that fit your abilities. If a health condition affects what is appropriate for you, discuss the type and amount with your clinician. Do not use this worksheet to override advice you have already received.

## Find three slots before choosing equipment

Open your calendar and look for repeatable opportunities. “After lunch on Tuesday” is more actionable than “exercise more.” Account for changing clothes, travel and returning to the next task; a 20-minute opening does not necessarily fit a 20-minute gym session.

For each slot, write four things:

1. **When and where:** A real opening and an accessible location.
2. **Activity:** Something you can do with equipment you already have.
3. **Fallback:** A shorter version or another suitable time if plans change.
4. **Review note:** What made the slot practical or difficult.

Do not assume that the fallback has the same duration, intensity or training effect as the original activity. It simply helps you make a deliberate decision when the day changes.

## Worked example: a busy beginner's planning week

This is an invented calendar example, not a participant result, a complete fitness programme or a schedule everyone should follow.

| Slot | Planned activity | If the slot disappears |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Monday after lunch | 15-minute walk on a familiar route | Consider a shorter walk later if suitable |
| Wednesday before dinner | 15-minute walk with a friend | Choose another agreed day rather than rushing |
| Saturday morning | 20-minute walk near home | Use an accessible indoor route if appropriate |

The planned total is **50 minutes**, not 150, and this example includes no muscle-strengthening sessions. Make that gap visible rather than relabelling a small starting week as a complete programme. After reviewing what worked, decide whether to change the timing, duration or activity. Add only what fits your abilities and circumstances.

Record what happened as a fact: “Walked 10 minutes; meeting ran late.” You do not need a red failure marker, a perfect streak or a score that combines unrelated activities. A cancelled session can reveal a scheduling problem rather than a lack of motivation.

## Review the calendar, not just the total

At the end of the week, ask which slot you would happily repeat, which repeatedly collided with another commitment, and whether preparation took longer than expected. Keep the workable slot. Move or remove the unreliable one before adding more sessions.

For example, if Wednesday failed twice because dinner preparation ran late, try a different time instead of writing the same plan again. If meeting a friend made Monday easier to arrange, agree on the next date. These are planning experiments, not guarantees about forming a habit in a fixed number of days.

## What you do not need to buy

You can start this worksheet in a notebook or a free notes app. It does not require a smartwatch, subscription, supplement or new skincare routine. Buy equipment only when a particular activity requires it and you know what problem it will solve.

If training changes when you shower or use your usual skincare, see our [skincare and training calendar](/skincare-and-training-calendar/). Keep that routine separate from your movement target; buying cosmetics is not evidence of exercise progress.
