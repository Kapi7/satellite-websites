A Simple Weekly Review for Food, Movement, and Rest

A weekly review is a simple tool for self-awareness in well-being. This guide offers a framework for reflecting on food, movement, and rest patterns, helping you identify what works and where small adjustments might help. The aim is consistent, gentle observation and planning, not perfection.

### Why a Weekly Review?
In busy lives, routines can go unexamined. A weekly review offers a moment to pause and observe. Regular check-ins help you:
*   **Identify patterns**: Notice habit trends.
*   **Prevent overwhelm**: Address small issues early.
*   **Foster intentionality**: Make conscious choices about nourishment, activity, and restoration.
*   **Support planning**: Use past insights for future plans.

### The Three Pillars: Food, Movement, and Rest

### Food: Nourishment and Planning
Reviewing your food intake focuses on understanding patterns, not judgment. Consider:
*   **Variety**: Did you consume a diverse range of foods (fruits, vegetables, whole grains, protein)?
*   **Hydration**: Was water intake consistent throughout the day?
*   **Meal Rhythm**: How consistent were meal times? Did you feel adequately fueled?
*   **Preparation**: What food preparation worked well? What could be planned more effectively?

### Movement: Activity and Enjoyment
Movement includes formal exercise and daily physical activity. Review:
*   **Consistency**: How often did you move your body?
*   **Type**: Did you engage in varied activities (e.g., walking, stretching, strength-focused)?
*   **Enjoyment**: What movement felt good or positive?
*   **Barriers**: What prevented movement as intended?

### Rest: Restoration and Recharge
Rest includes sleep, relaxation, and downtime, crucial for restoration. Evaluate:
*   **Sleep Quality**: How consistent was sleep? Did you wake refreshed?
*   **Downtime**: Did you create space for relaxation or quiet reflection?
*   **Energy Levels**: How were your energy levels? Were there dips?
*   **Stress Management**: What strategies helped manage stressors?

### How to Conduct Your Review
Choose a consistent 15-30 minute slot each week, like Sunday afternoon or Monday morning. In a quiet space, with your preferred notes, use the checklist. Focus on observation, not self-criticism.

### Making Adjustments
From your review, pick one or two small, actionable adjustments for the next week. Avoid overhauling everything. Small, consistent steps are more sustainable than drastic changes. For example, instead of "eat perfectly," try "add one more vegetable to dinner three times this week" or "drink water before each meal."

### Limitations
This self-assessment tool fosters personal awareness and supports planning. It is not intended to diagnose, treat, or prevent health conditions. For specific health concerns, dietary needs, or persistent sleep issues, consult a qualified healthcare professional. This guide focuses on organizational and planning, not medical advice.
