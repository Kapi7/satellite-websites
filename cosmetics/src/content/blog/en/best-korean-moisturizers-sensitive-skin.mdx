There is no Korean moisturizer we can promise will never sting, cause a reaction or clog pores. Start with your known triggers, the current ingredient label and a texture you will use consistently. Country of origin and a “sensitive skin” label cannot settle that decision.

## Start with the formula, not a ranking

The [American Academy of Dermatology’s dry-skin guidance](https://www.aad.org/public/everyday-care/skin-care-basics/dry/dermatologists-tips-relieve-dry-skin) recommends fragrance-free products and explains that creams or ointments can be more useful than lotions for dry skin. “Unscented” is not the same assurance as “fragrance-free.” A botanical ingredient is not automatically suitable just because it is natural.

Those principles help narrow a shortlist; they do not establish that every rich cream suits every person with sensitive skin. If a clinician has identified an ingredient allergy, check the full label for that ingredient rather than relying on a highlighted “soothing” ingredient.

| Your question | What to check |
| --- | --- |
| Does it contain a known trigger? | The complete current ingredient list, including fragrance and botanical ingredients |
| Is this the same product I used before? | Exact name, formula, package size and any reformulation notice |
| Will I use this texture? | Manufacturer description and a small trial, rather than an invented tester verdict |
| Is the larger size better value? | Price per unit and the amount you expect to use |

## Check sizes and look-alike names carefully

“Centella Cream” and “Centella Soothing Cream” should not be assumed to identify the same formula. Likewise, a product photograph of a jar does not establish that a listing for a tube contains the same package. Compare the full name, size and ingredient label before ordering.

We removed earlier size-mismatched photographs from this guide. Do not use the old images to choose between ETUDE or SKIN1004 packages. If the photograph and written listing disagree, resolve that discrepancy with the retailer before paying.

## Introduce it cautiously

Follow the product instructions. The [AAD’s product-testing guide](https://www.aad.org/public/everyday-care/skin-care-secrets/prevent-skin-problems/test-skin-care-products) describes testing a normal amount on a small area twice daily for seven to ten days. Stop using a product that causes a reaction; persistent or severe symptoms deserve medical advice. A home test is not the same as diagnostic patch testing by a clinician.
