A useful skincare shopping list starts with the jobs you need your products to do. Before comparing another serum, check whether your routine already covers cleansing, moisturizing, and sun protection. This guide is a shopping framework, not a hands-on product test.

## Start with three jobs

The American Academy of Dermatology recommends a simple approach built around a cleanser, moisturizer, and sun protection when managing skincare costs. A higher price does not automatically make a product more effective. See the [AAD's guidance on skincare on a budget](https://www.aad.org/public/everyday-care/skin-care-basics/care/skin-care-budget).

| Job | What to check before buying | What can wait |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Cleanse | Product instructions, texture, and whether your current cleanser feels comfortable | A second cleanser that duplicates the first |
| Moisturize | A texture you will use consistently and the full ingredient list | Multiple creams for the same part of your routine |
| Protect | Broad-spectrum, water-resistant SPF 30 or higher; follow the label | Buying a serum before replacing an empty sunscreen |

This is a starting point. Keep treatments prescribed for you and follow your clinician's instructions rather than replacing them with this list.

## Write down what you already own

Make three columns on your phone: product, job, and amount remaining. Put each bottle in one main category. Two hydrating essences may feel different, but that does not mean both are necessary for your routine.

A practical example: if you have a cleanser you like, two half-full moisturizers, and an almost-empty sunscreen, the immediate shopping priority is sunscreen. A discounted moisturizer is still an extra purchase. Using up products you already tolerate is often the simplest way to spend less.

## Compare the same units

Compare price per 100 ml within the same product category. Divide the price by the volume in milliliters and multiply by 100. A hypothetical €15 cleanser containing 150 ml costs €10 per 100 ml; a €12 cleanser containing 100 ml costs €12 per 100 ml. These numbers are examples, not retailer prices.

Unit price is only one filter. A large bottle is poor value if you dislike it, and a small travel size may make sense when trying a new texture. Do not compare the volume of sunscreen and serum as if they serve the same purpose.

## Add one new product at a time

The AAD suggests testing a new product on a small area twice daily for seven to ten days, using the amount and contact time specified for that product. Wash it off and stop using it if it causes a reaction. This home check does not rule out every possible reaction. Read the [full product-testing instructions](https://www.aad.org/public/everyday-care/skin-care-secrets/prevent-skin-problems/test-skin-care-products).
