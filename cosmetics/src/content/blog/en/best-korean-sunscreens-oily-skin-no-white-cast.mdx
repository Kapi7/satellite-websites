## What “no white cast” does and does not tell you

A retailer's no-white-cast description is not a test across every skin tone. Look for demonstrations that identify the exact formula, shade of skin, amount applied and lighting. A heavily filtered video or a very thin application is weak evidence for how a protective application will look on you.

Likewise, “lightweight” does not establish water resistance, acne safety or a matte finish. If swimming or sweating is part of your plans, choose a product whose own label states the water resistance you need.

## Use matters more than a perfect texture claim

The [American Academy of Dermatology](https://www.aad.org/public/everyday-care/sun-protection/shade-clothing-sunscreen/choosing-right-sunscreen) recommends broad-spectrum SPF 30 or higher and water resistance, alongside shade and protective clothing. Follow the product's directions, use an adequate amount and reapply at least every two hours outdoors, and after swimming or sweating as directed. A cushion foundation's SPF label should not be used to justify skipping a dedicated sunscreen application.

If layering causes pilling, reassess the products underneath and follow their directions; reducing sunscreen to a tiny amount is not a reliable fix. For persistent irritation, stop using the product and seek individual advice.
