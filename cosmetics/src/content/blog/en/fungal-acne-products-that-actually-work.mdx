If recurring bumps are making you replace your skincare repeatedly, pause before buying another routine marketed as “fungal-acne safe.” A product list cannot diagnose the cause of a rash or establish that a cosmetic treats it.

## What the term means—and what it cannot tell you

[DermNet's clinician-authored explanation](https://dermnetnz.org/topics/malassezia-folliculitis) describes Malassezia folliculitis as a yeast-associated condition that can resemble acne. Similar-looking bumps can have different causes; assessment may include examining or testing skin samples. Appearance or an online ingredient checker alone is not a diagnosis.

A clinician can advise whether antifungal treatment is appropriate. Do not stop prescribed medication or start a face-treatment schedule based on this shopping guide. Painful, worsening or persistent symptoms deserve individual medical assessment.

## Take a useful product list to your appointment

Record the names of your cleanser, moisturizer, sunscreen and any treatments, along with when each was introduced. Keep the actual ingredient lists or packaging available. Note where symptoms occur and whether a new product seems to irritate your skin. This is more useful than bringing a long list of products labelled safe by an automated checker.

Questions to ask include whether the diagnosis is clear, which products should stay in your routine, and what signs should prompt follow-up. The answer may involve buying fewer cosmetics.
